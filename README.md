## Overview

A crisis apparition is a specific type of spontaneous apparition in which a person appears to friends, family, or loved ones at the moment of — or shortly before or after — their death, serious injury, or extreme distress, despite being physically distant from the witness. Unlike traditional ghosts that appear long after death at haunted locations, crisis apparitions occur as one-time events, typically within 12 hours of the crisis itself. These phenomena have been extensively documented by psychical researchers since the late 19th century and represent one of the most statistically well-attested categories of spontaneous paranormal experience. The Society for Psychical Research (SPR), founded in 1882, conducted systematic surveys and found that crisis apparitions occurred 440 times more frequently than chance expectation. In 1886, Edmund Gurney, Frederic W.H. Myers, and Frank Podmore published Phantasms of the Living, a 1,300-page two-volume analysis of 702 verified apparition cases collected from 5,700 respondents. The dominant psychical-research theory holds that crisis apparitions are hallucinations caused by telepathic impressions from the distressed person reaching the percipient's mind. Mainstream psychology attributes such reports to coincidence, selective memory, grief, and the human tendency to impose narrative meaning on random events.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Telepathic transmission** _(Edmund Gurney, Frederic W.H. Myers, Frank Podmore)_

The distress of the dying or endangered person generates a telepathic impulse that reaches the mind of the percipient, producing a hallucination of the agent. This was the dominant theory of the SPR founders.

**Survival of consciousness / communication from the dying** _(Spiritualist tradition, Some parapsychologists)_

The consciousness of the dying or seriously distressed person partially separates from the body and projects itself to loved ones.

### Scientific

**Coincidence with grief hallucination** _(W. Dewi Rees (1971 bereavement hallucination study), Julian Jaynes)_

Bereavement and extreme stress regularly produce hallucinations of deceased or absent loved ones; when these coincide with an actual crisis, survivors retrospectively connect them.

### Skeptical

**Selective memory and retrospective fabrication** _(Susan Blackmore, Michael Shermer)_

People who dream of or think of an absent loved one many times never note it; on the rare occasion this coincides with a genuine crisis, they report it as a crisis apparition. Selection and memory distortion explain the apparent frequency.
